# Batch 01 · First Ford

One battlefield and ten card illustrations for the playable opening of **Battle Master — The Bell Marches**. This set covers every possible opening deck, all four tactics and the promoted swordsman used in the practice clash.

Generate **Long Swordsman** with the approved Swordsman image attached as a character reference. Use each remaining prompt on its own, one image per generation. ### 01 · Swordsman · `sword1.png`

*A shield you would stand behind*

```text
Paint the first Sword regiment of the Bell Marches. A compact, broad-shouldered guard has stepped down from a wagon to wait at the ford. Frame the guard from mid-thigh upward, turned three quarters to the right, looking just past us. The visual anchor is an indigo, round-cornered square shield with a single wide ivory stripe and one repaired copper corner. The shield sits below the face. A plain, short sword points down at the guard’s side, its tip fully inside the composition. Give the guard a straight dark fringe, a weathered brown face and a short rust scarf tied in a square knot at the left shoulder. One blurred wagon wheel behind the far elbow sets the scale. Use a warm ivory sky and a blue-grey river shadow. It should feel like meeting the person who will guard your wagon through five crossings.

Create one full-bleed portrait illustration, 5:7, with an opaque background. Target a 1500 × 2100 px or larger master. The Bell Marches is a fictional river frontier of travelling wagon-forts. Use a contemporary editorial woodcut on dyed paper: bold shapes, crisp carved edges, subtle paper tooth visible up close, three clear light values. Use deep indigo ink, warm ivory paper, muted river teal and sparing burnished copper. Keep the focal object or face clear at thumbnail size. Leave roughly 18% headroom and 12% breathing room on both sides. Keep essential faces, hands and identifying details clear of the upper corners and bottom 15%, where the game frame may overlap. Deliver the illustration alone. Omit letters, numbers, signatures, logos, card borders, stat badges, UI and watermarks.
```

## Battlefield prompt

### 11 · First Ford · `first_ford.png`

```text
Create one full-bleed 16:9 landscape environment painting for the First Ford duel in Battle Master: The Bell Marches. Target an opaque 3200 × 1800 px master. View a shallow crossing from the rising bank beside a travelling wagon-fort, looking toward the opposite shore in cool early morning. A low belt of apricot light catches the distant willow crowns and a tiny copper ford bell. The wagon ruts on the viewer’s left descend into smooth silver-blue water; on their right a gentle raised spine of dry ground crests above the ford. These two natural routes give Road and Ridge a geographical identity. Let the river flow behind both of them as one continuous landscape.

Compose for a landscape phone. Put the horizon at the upper third. Keep the left and middle two-thirds, where most regiment slots sit, broad and low-contrast: pale water, open silt and soft shadow. Keep the rightmost quarter, under the command panel, quiet as well. Place the little landmark bell and a hint of a distant covered wagon near the far shoreline, clear of the all-important route silhouettes. Frame only the outer corners with a dark reed cluster and the cropped curve of a wagon wheel. The scene must survive losing 8% from either side. A few copper glints carry the eye across the water; no scatter of sparkling highlights behind cards.

Render this as a modern panoramic woodcut printed on dyed paper. Use large interlocking shapes in indigo, muted teal and warm ivory, with one restrained copper accent. Fine directional cuts follow the bank; the water stays mostly flat. Atmospheric layers should give depth while the silhouettes stay clean. Show the ford just before the companies arrive. Omit crowds, foreground characters, lettering, numbers, banners with writing, card outlines, slots, grids, lane dividers, HUD, logo, frame and watermark.
```

## Review before accepting the masters

- **Frame fit.** Place a temporary arch over each portrait. Mask the top corners and bottom 15%. Check the spear point, sword tip, archer’s bow tips, horse’s ears and hooves, and both hands in the courier exchange. If a feature touches the mask, widen the composition in the next generation.
- **Small-screen read.** View cards at 90 px wide. The sword’s square shield, archer’s crescent, spear’s upright line, rider’s horse and levy’s round shield should distinguish the recruits even in greyscale. Check that the weave on Wicker Screens still reads as one solid panel.
- **Promotion continuity.** Compare `sword1` and `sword2` side by side. The face, scarf knot and repaired shield corner should match. The longer blade and forward step communicate the change of form.
- **Battlefield fit.** Place a mock board over First Ford at 844 × 390 and 667 × 375 with both fort totals, two lanes, command panel and hand visible. Check that the banks still explain Road and Ridge and that high-contrast details do not compete with unit names.
